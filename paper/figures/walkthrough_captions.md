Panel texts of the walkthrough figure, moved out of the figure (generated by fig_walkthrough.py).

- **(1) Quantise positions.** Each coordinate is rounded down to an integer k in [0, 2³⁰), and the position is k × step: positions are quantised to integer coordinates on a 2³⁰ grid (inset: one grid cell, much enlarged). Every later decision is an exact test on these integer positions; exact ties break by particle id. *Code:* exact_predicates.hpp, voronoi_mesh_2d.hpp (quantisation)
- **(2) Morton cell list.** Sorted along a Morton curve (line, slot 0 to 95): each tree node is one run of slots. Each particle's level has about one particle per cell: 4 (16², dark blue) in the patch, 3 (8², light blue) around. *Code:* neighbor_search.hpp: configure, build
- **(3) Clip one cell.** From a square of half-width L/4, cut by bisectors nearest first (numbers). The walk stops at the first grid cell 2R away (dashed), R the farthest corner: 7 cuts, 1 miss (hollow), 87 never read. *Code:* polygon_clip.hpp: construct_pass; 3D: shell_voronoi_warp.hpp
- **(4) All cells: face rings.** Every cell is clipped on its own, in parallel, and keeps its ring: its neighbours in order (q1 … q7 here), in one pool. The rings are the whole topology; no corner is stored. *Code:* polygon_clip.hpp: construct; 3D: shell_voronoi.hpp, per face
- **(5) Exact start.** Consecutive ring entries give triangles (i, qₘ, qₘ₊₁), each once: 192 = 2n, with 3n = 288 faces. Checked: the exact screen and flips change 0 of them; the areas add up to the box once. *Code:* tri_mesh.hpp: from_rings; 3D: tet_start.hpp: build, check
- **(6) Points move.** The solver calls update(x) with new positions, here up to 0.25 mean spacings (arrows ×3). The triangles are kept (grey); a step needs no cell list and no clipping. *Code:* voronoi_mesh_2d.hpp: Mesh::update; (the caller: luna-cpp, every step)
- **(7) Screen.** One pass over the old triangles at the new points: the in-circle test of each edge in double, with a rounding bound. Failing, unsure, and (in so small a box) long edges are listed: 42 of 288. *Code:* tri_repair.hpp: screen; 3D: tet_repair.hpp: move_screened
- **(8) Flip.** The exact test decides each listed edge. Flips (dashed: gone, green: new) run in rounds; votes settle conflicts, so thread order is moot. 13 flips in 6 rounds; volumes equal a fresh build's. *Code:* tri_repair.hpp: repair; 3D: tet_repair.hpp: flip23, flip32
- **(9) Geometry: the dual.** Circumcentres (blue dots) are the cell corners: the Voronoi mesh is the dual of the triangles. One thread per face writes its area vector (arrows) and centroid (orange). *Code:* tri_mesh.hpp: faces; 3D: tet_mesh.hpp: faces
- **(10) update_cells().** Per cell, a list of its faces sorted by the other particle's id (1 … 7). Volume and centroid (×) are summed over the pyramids in that order: the same bits on every run. Σ volume = L² exactly here. *Code:* tet_cells.hpp: cells; (the same code in 2D and 3D)
- **(11) Fallbacks.** Two particles jump (arrows); flips leave 1 inverted triangle (red). Few stuck: the host redoes a patch (11 triangles), the GPU flips again. Many: a rebuild (1–5). Two at one integer position: refused. *Code:* tri_finish.hpp: finish (host); 3D: tet_flips.hpp: finish
- **(12) Re-sort (3D).** Over many steps ids drift apart in space (grey: id order). Renumbering along a Hilbert curve (colour) restores locality: the mean id gap over faces is 13 at the start, 24 now, 13 after. *Code:* mesh_order.hpp: cell_order; voronoi_mesh.hpp: Mesh::resort
