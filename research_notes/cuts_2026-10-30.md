# Faster cuts in the warp kernel (2026-10-30)

Goal: make the plane cuts of the warp-per-cell clipping kernel
(`shell_voronoi_warp.hpp`) faster on an A100 40 GB in double precision, with
the same cells. Changes are confined to `Polyhedron`, `start_cube`, `clip()`
and the vertex loops of the certification and face output; the stencil,
grid walk, candidate fetch and sort are untouched, except six lines of the
flush loop in commit 9.

## Profile of main (3074c1e)

Per-phase clock64 counters (a profiling-only patch, not committed; it costs
about 5 %), 2.1M cells, share of warp cycles:

| | walk | fetch | sort | test, no cut | cut | output | cycles per cut | per test without cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| jittered (24.3 tests, 16.2 cuts per cell) | 7 % | 3 % | 5 % | 7 % | 63 % | 15 % | 5890 | 1280 |
| uniform (47.9 tests, 17.8 cuts) | 7 % | 5 % | 8 % | 15 % | 52 % | 13 % | 8010 | 1310 |
| clustered (97 tests, 42 cuts) | 3 % | 8 % | 8 % | 15 % | 59 % | 8 % | 6410 | 1240 |

Nsight Compute, jittered: 96 registers (the 5-blocks cap), 31 KB shared
per block of 4 warps, 5 blocks per SM limited by registers *and* shared
memory, i.e. 20 warps per SM (theoretical = achieved occupancy 31.25 %;
the 16.7 % in the status note was measured before the register cap), 15.2 of 32
lanes active, 0.54 instructions issued per scheduler and cycle, L1 hit rate
31 % (the shared memory carve-out leaves little L1), 34 G instructions,
80 MB of register spills.

What a cut cost: every cut copied all kept vertices (positions, corners,
adjacency) into the second buffer and renumbered their edges; a lane built
the new vertices of its removed vertices one after the other (up to six,
each with a double division); a square root per plane test; a five-round
double shuffle for the radius; and two ballots, one per vertex half, wherever
the warp voted.

## Changes (one commit each)

1. **In-place cuts** (89e028a). Vertices stay in their slots; new vertices go
   into free slots (a stack of slot numbers in shared memory), the kept end
   of each crossed edge is rewired by a byte store, and a 64-bit mask in
   registers marks the slots in use. No second vertex copy, no replacement
   table, no renumbering. 7.7 -> 5.5 KB per polyhedron.
2. **Lane j builds new vertex j** (5d8d351). Crossed edges are numbered by one
   ballot per edge and listed in shared memory; lane j builds new vertex j
   (and j + 32): one division per lane instead of up to six in a row.
3. **Tolerance in squares** (21aa681). `side > 0 && side^2 > (64 eps)^2 |d|^2 R^2`
   instead of a square root per plane test; the cut's trailing `__syncwarp`
   dropped (the next cut and the face output synchronise before reading).
4. **Crossed edges listed with both ends and planes** (43be669): the lane
   building a new vertex needs no second round of shared loads.
5. **Float plane normals, no plane offsets** (71204bf). Offsets were never
   read; normals only weight the triangles of a face for its centroid, and
   every triangle of a face sees the same rounded normal. 4.5 KB per
   polyhedron, 18 KB shared per block.
6. **Skip the edge ballots of an empty vertex half** (648c8bf): slots 32..63
   rarely hold a removed vertex; the warp skips their three ballots.
7. **Radius by integer warp reductions** (06a08fd). Squared distances are
   non-negative, so their bit patterns order like unsigned integers: two
   `__reduce_max_sync` (high words, then low words of the lanes holding the
   maximum high word) give exactly the double maximum.
8. **New slots join by OR reductions** (dfa3266): builders OR their slot bits
   (two `__reduce_or_sync`) instead of owners detecting new vertices by
   their plane and voting; the kept radius is taken in the plane test.
9. **Sorted candidates loaded by all lanes at once** (96ec75a). After the
   sort lane i loads candidate i; the clip loop broadcasts the offset with
   three shuffles instead of a dependent global load per candidate. This is
   the only change in `Kernel::build` outside the clip call (six lines after
   `sort_candidates`); it can be dropped if it conflicts with the adaptive
   cell list.

The surprise is how much the warp-collective chain costs: removing three
ballots (6) or replacing five shuffle rounds by two reductions (7) each gave
5-7 %, as much as the square root (3). A cut is a dependent chain of
votes, shuffles and `__syncwarp`s, and with 5 warps per scheduler there is
little to hide their latency behind.

## Exactness

`cut_dump` (a scratch tool: status, sorted owned neighbours and volume of
every cell, compared between builds) against main on 2.1M cells: no status
differs, no neighbour set differs, largest relative volume difference
3.0e-15 (jittered), 6.1e-15 (uniform), 4.8e-15 (clustered); these come
from the face walk starting at another vertex (slots are numbered
differently). `voronoi_bench` cell checks give the same results as main
(jittered and clustered PASS; uniform shows the known face-pair FAIL of
clipping alone, unchanged). ctest 4/4 on the A100 (job 31686668) and on
the CPU (OpenMP, gcc 15); clang-tidy clean.

One behaviour difference: new vertices only take slots that were free
before the cut, so a cut overflows when in-use + new > 64 (main: kept + new
> 64). The few cells this affects are rebuilt by the thread kernel, as
before; their results are the same, only the work counters of those cells
differ (rings per cell 6.33216 vs 6.33222 on uniform).

## Tried and dropped (measured in the same job as their baseline)

| Variant | jittered | uniform | Why dropped |
|---|---|---|---|
| Register cache of each lane's own vertex positions (plane test from registers) | -4 % | -4 % | 9x more spill traffic (694 vs 80 MB) at the 96-register cap |
| Lazy radius (refreshed only when a batch would stop, and after it) | -1 % | 0 | the tolerance still uses a stale, larger radius; no gain |
| Candidates loaded at once, together with the lazy radius (before 6-8) | 0 | -1 % | became +2 % once the cut chain was shorter (commit 9) |
| Owned faces kept per plane slot (no runtime-indexed arrays), numbered by ballots | 0 | 0 | stack 224 -> 136 B, no time change |
| One ballot for the plane test when slots 32.. are empty | -2 % | -2 % | slower; not investigated further |
| 6 blocks per SM (80 registers), before 6-8 / after 8 | -1 % / -5 % | -3 % / -5 % | more spills; shared memory would allow 8 blocks |
| 4 blocks per SM (128 registers) | -1 % | -2 % | fewer warps |

## Open items

- The kernel stays latency-bound: 5 warps per scheduler, 39 % of cycles
  with no eligible warp. Registers (96, with spills) cap the occupancy;
  shared memory no longer does (18 KB per block would allow 8 blocks).
  Cutting register pressure in the walk/fetch part would let 6 blocks run.
- Divergence: 21 of 32 lanes active (main 15). Cuts are now half of the
  jittered warp cycles; the face output (one face walked per lane, nine
  double warp sums) is 21 % and the next target, then the candidate sort.
- Clustered input gains only 5 % because the warp kernel is a small part of
  its time: under Nsight the warp kernel takes 392 ms on main and 238 ms on
  the branch (1.65x, like the other inputs), of about 2.2 s in total; the
  other ~1.9 s is the thread-kernel pass over the cells the warp kernel
  leaves (uncertified within its stencil reach, i.e. cells in voids, or
  overflowing). The adaptive cell list is the lever there.
- Without diagnostics (`Cells(n, false)`), area_sum/area_magnitude warp sums
  in the face output could be skipped (four double warp reductions per
  cell); not done since the benchmark keeps diagnostics on.
- The profiling patch (per-phase clock64 counters) and the dump/compare tool
  used here live in `build-cuts/tools/` on Raven, not in the repository.
