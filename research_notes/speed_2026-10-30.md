# Speed of the per-step mesh update, 2026-10-30 (branch `worktree-agent-a07689b0a6216e0d8`)

Goal: a faster persistent-mesh step on one A100 40 GB in double precision,
exact results, lean code. Scope: the per-step path (tet_repair, tet_mesh
rings/faces/centres, tet_flips::finish, voronoi_bench step); the rebuild
kernels, the cell list and `tet_mesh::from_rings` were left to other work.
All numbers: 2,097,152 jittered cells, median of steps 2-10, variants
interleaved within one gpu1 job; logs and nsys reports in
`/raven/u/uli/voronoi-cpp/build-speed/logs/`.

## Where the time went (main, 0.03 spacings, nsys)

| kernel(s) | ms/step |
|---|---|
| `update_face_rings` (2 kernels, 2 scans) | 5.5 |
| `faces` | 3.6 |
| `move_screened` (screen) | 2.4 |
| `circumcentres` (twice) | 2.1 |
| `repair` rounds (~35 small kernels, 6 rounds) | 2.1 |
| pad, snap, memsets | 1.0 |
| complete step (incl. host round trips) | 16.7 |

Host round trips in `repair` (two pinned counter reads per round) cost
~0.3 ms per step, so a graph or device-side loop (lever 2) was not worth it.

At 0.1 and 0.3 spacings the picture is different: most steps leave 2-7
stuck triangles, and `tet_flips::finish` took **5.6-7 s per step**: it
copied the whole mesh (capacity rows) to the host twice and back, and
`tet_flips::repair` queued every tetrahedron (~60 M exact predicates on
one core). The README's "~35 ms at 0.3" left this out.

## What changed (one commit each)

1. **Circumcentres after the repair only for touched tetrahedra**
   (positions do not change between repair and geometry).
2. **Face rings in place** (`FaceRings` holds both step buffers and a ring
   pool): a clean face takes its old (ring_start, ring_length); faces with a
   touched tetrahedron on their ring are found from the touched tetrahedra
   (walk each edge to its representative) and their rings appended to the
   pool, which is compacted into a spare pool when full. Clean rings are
   never read or copied. `Mesh` gained `ring_length`. `finish` marks
   tetrahedra whose edge bits it changes as touched (required: untouched
   tetrahedra keep their face numbering).
3. Circumcentres inside the screening kernel: **slower** (101 instead of 46
   registers; screen 2.4 -> 4.1 ms); fixed by 5.
4. **Compact lists.** Kernels over all tetrahedra with ~1 % active threads
   cost nearly as much as dense ones (one busy lane keeps its warp: the
   touched-only circumcentres took 0.63 of 1.29 ms, the dirty-face search
   0.8 ms, the ring scan that walked 7 % of the faces 1.55 ms). The repair
   now lists the tetrahedra it creates (`State::touched_list`, -1 entries
   when it overflows or after a whole-mesh host repair); circumcentres and
   the dirty-face search run over that list, dirty faces are listed once
   (atomic exchange on their marker) and their lengths, pool positions and
   walks run over that list.
5. **Slivers out of the screen**: slivers are suspect anyway, so the screen
   computes the double circumcentre itself and lists slivers; a second pass
   computes their exact centres. Values unchanged; the separate
   circumcentres pass before the screen is gone.
6. Kept rings written in the final pass of the face-numbering scan (no
   separate face_lengths pass, no marker clearing).
7. **Host fallback on the region around the stuck entries**: the
   tetrahedra within 8 adjacency steps (device BFS with the repair's
   mark/list) are repaired on the host as a local mesh (particles renumbered
   in the same order, so every exact decision, including symbolic
   perturbation, is the same; faces to the outside open). Changed and new
   rows go back by a scatter kernel, outside neighbours are reconnected by
   face key, edges around them get representatives on the device (same
   lowest-number rule), everything touched is listed, and the device repair
   runs from the changed and the stuck tetrahedra to settle the border.
   The old whole-mesh path is kept as `finish_everywhere`, used when a local
   result does not fit its border.
8. **`wrap` multiplies by 1 / box** instead of dividing: the faces kernel
   was partly bound by double divisions (3.37 -> 2.86 ms). Same image unless
   a difference is within an ulp of half a box; bitwise identical volumes in
   all checks.
9. The device recheck after the local repair also starts from the stuck
   entries (otherwise a triangle the host could not repair either went
   unnoticed and finish reported success; caught by the instrumented run).
10. Marking walks stop at a touched tetrahedron numbered below their start
    (that one marks the face; the lowest touched one on a ring always gets
    through), and the repair lists a created tetrahedron only the first time
    it is touched in a step (slots from the free list always). At 0.3
    spacings the marking pass took 5.5-8.3 ms per step.
11. `finish` stops after regions of 8 and 16 steps (see below).

## Correctness

ctest 4/4 (CPU OpenMP and CUDA on the A100) and clang-tidy clean after every
commit. `voronoi_bench step 262144 jittered 0.3 10 --verify` on the A100
prints exactly what main prints: failing 0 every step, max relative volume
error 1.0-1.2e-14, and the known 4/2/2 cells with one extra rounding-level
face at steps 1, 6 and 8 (see the cleanup note). On CPU (32768 cells,
jittered 0.3 and 0.03, uniform 0.1 and 0.3, sc@1e-3, fcc@1e-4, bcc@1e-2) the
volume errors are the same as on main, mostly bit for bit. 32768 jittered at
0.03 has a 1.45e-9 volume error at step 2 on main and here alike.

Found on the way, not caused here: at 2.1M cells and 0.3 spacings the mesh
is wrong on main already (`from_rings` reports 4 unresolved slots; with
`--verify` main shows mismatched cells and volume errors ~7 from step 1, and
2-3 stuck triangles per step that neither the device nor the host can
repair, failing 4-6 after main's whole-mesh host repair). This branch gives
the same failing counts. The exact starting tetrahedralization (other work)
should remove them; until then `finish` spends ~35 ms (8 steps) + ~150 ms (16
steps) per step on host rebuilds that fail, which is most of its time at 0.3.

## Memory

Face rings: per face +1 int (ring_length in both buffers, the dirty list;
owner/neighbour no longer double-buffered), the per-step face_length
temporary (faces ints) is gone; `touched_list` adds capacity / 4 ints. At
2.1M cells: about +110 MB permanent, -67 MB transient. The two ring pools
(8 x face capacity each) are as before; the spare one is only needed for
compaction (every ~10 steps at 0.03) and could be made smaller or temporary.

## Tried and dropped

- Circumcentres fused into the screen with the exact sliver path inlined
  (commit 3, fixed by 5): 101 registers, slower.
- Faces kernel with launch bounds (<=64 registers): no change (3.35 vs 3.37
  ms). Without the volume atomics: no change (atomics are free here).
- CUDA graph / device-side round loop for the repair (lever 2): the
  measured host overhead is ~0.3 ms per step at 0.03; not done.
- Nsight Compute: the cuda/13.0 module's `ncu` lacks its nsight-compute
  directory on Raven; A/B builds were used instead.

## What is left

- `faces` (2.9 ms) and the screen (~2.6 ms) are now the largest kernels at
  0.03; both are gathers of 32-byte centres/positions. A per-tetrahedron
  formulation of the face sums or a lighter screen (e.g. skip tetrahedra
  whose vertices barely moved) are the next candidates.
- Repair rounds (2.2 ms at 0.03, ~20 ms at 0.3, 15-19 rounds): plan and find
  use 132-152 registers; at 0.3 the round count dominates.
- At 0.3 the ring update still walks many faces (dirty fraction is large):
  the dirty-length and append passes walk each dirty face twice.
- The unrepairable triangles at 2.1M/0.3 (see Correctness) and the cost of
  `tet_flips::repair`'s failing rebuild attempts (35-170 ms).
- `tet_flips::repair` queues every tetrahedron of its mesh; with local
  meshes that no longer matters, but `finish_everywhere` still pays it.

## Addendum: two verification failures after the merge (branch `fix-verify`)

After merging this work with the exact start (main fa6b61c), `--verify` at
262144 particles failed on wave (step 1, failing 24/34) and clustered (one
face short every step, volume error growing to 3.5e-5). Root causes:

1. `tet_repair::repair` silently dropped failing triangles beyond the work
   capacity and stuck entries beyond the stuck capacity (wave, 4096
   particles, 0.3 spacings: 22213 failing triangles for 15517 slots in the
   first round, 38336 stuck entries for 15517). The dropped triangles' tets
   were already unlisted and never checked again. The whole-mesh host
   fallback used to recheck every tetrahedron and hid this; the local
   fallback trusts the lists. Fixed by growing the lists (1b1c053). The
   "unrepairable" triangles at 2.1M/0.3 above were most likely the same
   overflow (with the inexact start); `finish` again ends with the
   whole-mesh repair if local regions leave stuck entries (ddd9bff), so it
   never leaves failing triangles.
2. `from_rings` took edge representatives only from the owners' clipped
   faces; an edge whose owner cell dropped a rounding-level face had none,
   so its Voronoi face was never computed (clustered 262144: 1 edge; wave
   32768: 59). Every such edge is now represented by the lowest-numbered
   tetrahedron around it (c238bc4); start_test checks one representative
   per edge, including wave at 32768.

A100 check (gpu1 job 31688232, ravg1005, code of ddd9bff): `step 262144
<kind> <d> 5 --verify` PASS for jittered, uniform, clustered, lattice and
wave at 0.03 and 0.3 (failing 0, no missing faces, max relative volume
error <= 4.8e-14; 0-2 extra faces per step are the known rounding-level
faces). Wave's first step leaves ~21500 stuck entries and takes 2.5 s on
the host. ctest 5/5. 2.1M jittered steps interleaved with fa6b61c (median
of steps 2-10, two rounds): 11.69 / 20.11 / 38.32 ms vs 11.63 / 20.14 /
38.27 ms at 0.03 / 0.1 / 0.3; the starting mesh costs ~5-10 ms more
(~465 -> 470-485 ms) for the dropped-face representatives.
