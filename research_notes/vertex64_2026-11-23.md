# #64: a vertex on a cut edge in the rounded clipping (measurement, 2026-11-23)

Branch `vertex-64`. Measurement only: nothing in the default build changes. The diagnostic is compiled in with
`-DVORONOI_VERTEX64=ON` (CMake option, off by default; `src/voronoi_construct/vertex64.hpp`).

## Where a new vertex comes from

- **Warp kernel** (`shell_voronoi_warp.hpp`, `clip`): `side[v] = d.x x_v + d.y y_v + d.z z_v − |d|²/2`, with `d` the
  offset from the generator to the cutting particle. A vertex is removed when `side > τ`, τ = 64 ε |d| R
  (ε = 2⁻⁵², R² = `radius2`, the largest squared vertex distance before the cut). The new vertex on the edge from removed
  `w` to kept `u` is `x_w + (x_u − x_w) t`, t = side[w] / (side[w] − side[u]).
- **Per-thread kernel** (`shell_cell.hpp`, `RoundedCell`; the CPU path and the GPU's overflow tail): the same cut
  test, but the new vertex is the meeting point of the three planes (`intersect`, Cramer's rule). It does not share the
  interpolation; it computes the same geometric point (the edge's line meets the plane), so the same degeneracy makes
  it ill-conditioned. Both are measured with the same side-based classification.
- **`clip()`** (`Mesh::clip`) builds `ExactCell`s (`construct(..., exact = true)`): no rounded vertex at all.
- **`Mesh` geometry** (rebuild, start, step) comes from the circumcentres of the exactly checked tetrahedra; the
  clipping's rounded vertices only feed the topology guess (`from_rings`), which `tet_start` checks exactly.

## The bound

For the stored doubles `d` and `x` (on the unit box `d` is exact: quantised positions are multiples of 2⁻³⁰), let
S = |d.x x| + |d.y y| + |d.z z| and o = |d|²/2. The computed side is a 3-term dot product (each product rounded or
fused) minus a computed offset (a 3-term sum of squares times the exact 0.5) and one subtraction. With
γ_k = k u / (1 − k u), u = 2⁻⁵³ (Higham, *Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms*, 2nd ed., §3.1):

    |fl(side) − side| ≤ γ_4 S + (γ_4 + γ_3 (1 + γ_4)) o ≤ γ_8 (S + o) < 9 u (S + o) =: B(x).

The code uses B = 9 u (S + o); the extra u covers rounding the bound itself.

**The strict flag cannot fire in the warp kernel.** A removed vertex has side[w] > 0, so d·x_w > |d|²/2 and
|d| < 2 |x_w| ≤ 2R; hence o < |d| R and S ≤ |d| R, so B(x_w) < 18 u |d| R. The cut needs side[w] > τ = 128 u |d| R
> 7 B(x_w). Both ends within rounding of the plane is therefore impossible by construction; the removal tolerance
already exceeds the rounding of side by a factor 7. The same holds for `RoundedCell` (same τ).

What can happen instead:

1. **Kept end outside** (side[u] ∈ (0, τ]): the vertex u stays because it is within the tolerance, but lies outside
   the plane. Then t > 1: the new vertex is placed on the line beyond u. If side[w] − side[u] is small (both ends near
   τ), t is large and the vertex lands far from the edge. Counted as `kept_outside` (side[u] > 0) and `beyond_bound`
   (side[u] > B(x_u), outside for certain).
2. **Ill-conditioning.** To first order, t = s_w / (s_w − s_u) moves by
   δt ≤ (|s_u| B_w + |s_w| B_u) / (s_w − s_u)², so the vertex moves along the edge by up to
   E = |x_u − x_w| δt. Counted as `err9`, `err6`, `err3`: E > 10⁻⁹, 10⁻⁶, 10⁻³ R. This bounds only the rounding of
   this cut's side values, not the error the stored vertices already carry.

The exact comparison does not rely on the bound: flagged cells (any of strict, kept_outside, err9) are built again
with `ExactCell` and compared (volume, centroid, radius = largest exact vertex distance), and the worst cut's vertex is
compared with the exact meeting point of its three planes (`exact_predicates::circumcentre` on the quantised offsets).

`VERTEX64_ALL=1`, closure); fuzz: CPU 32903810 (#62 seeds), 32904150 (profiles small, long, medium), GPU 32905678

/raven/u/uli/voronoi-cpp-joblogs/vertex64/.

## What it says

- The issue's flag (both ends within rounding of the plane) cannot fire, by the bound above; measured 0 everywhere.
- The mechanism that does happen is a kept end within the removal tolerance outside the plane (t > 1): a vertex placed
  beyond its edge. It occurs at nearly every near-cospherical vertex and is almost always harmless; on input
  cospherical to 1e-9 it leaves a few cells wrong by up to a third of their volume. Every wrong cell measured has such
  a cut, but routing on it would send 65–95 % of a lattice's cells to `exact_where_failed`.
- A per-cell closure above 1e-12 (the warp kernel already sums the area vectors) caught 2677 of the 2678 wrong cells
  and flagged no cell on any other input; the one it misses (fcc@1e-9) has an exact volume, its centroid 2.4e-3 cell
  sizes off and its radius 12 % too large.
- Neither #58 nor #62 is explained by it.
